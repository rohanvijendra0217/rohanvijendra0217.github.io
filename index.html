Rebuilding the M&A intelligence engine on Vertex AI

Diagnosed hallucination failures in a legacy AI pipeline and designed its replacement end-to-end: a 5-stage, multi-model architecture spanning data ingestion, a 5-step Vertex AI prompt chain, and CMS publish. Specced and validated every model, temperature, and grounding decision myself; engineering implemented it to that spec.

Vertex AI System Design Grounding
0 errorsacross 2,000+ articles
Product insight

More structured data in means less room for a model to invent facts. That's why data completeness became a hard publish gate, not a nice-to-have.

View the pipeline architecture
  1. Ingest. Pull the latest acquisitions from three APIs, deduplicate against already-published pages, and require 5 mandatory + at least 14 of 18 additional data points per deal, a threshold I set and tuned specifically to cut hallucination before generation even starts.
  2. Draft. Two Gemini Flash-Lite prompts (temp 0.3) generate the Deal Summary and sector M&A activity, strictly bounded to the fetched data. No outside knowledge allowed at this stage.
  3. Ground & verify. Two Gemini Flash prompts (temp 0.1, Google Search grounding) add web-researched context and fact-check every claim against the original data, while a locked-date tag I designed protects the acquisition date from drifting during web grounding.
  4. Format. A final Gemini 2.0 Flash prompt (temp 0.3) compresses the verified content into a 600-word, Bloomberg-style brief.
  5. Publish. Automated hyperlinking, format validation, and CMS handoff: 5–30 briefs a day, zero human review.

Trade-offs

Coverage vs. accuracy Fewer pages, higher bar

Gated on 14/18 fields instead of publishing everything.

Speed vs. stability 5s delay, zero 429s

Slower runs, but the pipeline never breaks at scale.

Automation vs. certainty One field stays manual

Date locked instead of re-verified by web search.

When this fails

Too few related deals Page doesn't publish

<4 related deals = skip, not a thin page.

Invalid output Blocked pre-publish

Malformed content never goes live.

Mid-pipeline errors Smart reruns

429 reruns 1 step; other errors restart all.

My role: designed, prompt-engineered, and validated every stage of this pipeline, including the model/temperature choice per step and the two-layer date-protection mechanism above. Engineering built the AppScript automation to this spec.

Consolidating 8 duplicate buy widgets before they cost 3.5M pages their ranking

A stakeholder proposal called for 8 near-identical "buy document" banners per page: same checkout link, same copy with one sentence changed per entity. Built the case against it with SEO risk modeling, competitive benchmarking, and a mobile UX walkthrough, then got a consolidated design approved instead of overriding the call unilaterally.

Stakeholder Alignment SEO Risk UX Research
+21%indexing lift
Product insight

Eight banners weren't eight options. To Google and to a mobile user, they read as the same offer repeated eight times, and both punished it.

View how the case was built
  1. Diagnose. The proposal: 8 empty banners per page, each with a different document name, but every buy button routing to the same checkout page and near-identical copy differing by one sentence per entity.
  2. Quantify the risk. Modeled what 8 near-duplicate banners × 3.5M pages actually signals to Google: scaled content abuse and thin/sparse-page patterns, the two triggers most likely to cause deindexing and ranking loss.
  3. Build the evidence. Competitive benchmarking on how other platforms handle multi-offer CTAs, plus a mobile walkthrough showing the empty banners pushing every other page offering below the fold.
  4. Push back. Presented the case directly to stakeholders with the data, laid out multiple alternative designs, and recommended one, rather than shipping a fix unilaterally.
  5. Ship & measure. Consolidated single-widget design went live.

Alternatives considered

Masked data-teaser table Only 1–10% coverage

Data too sparse to use platform-wide.

Redesigned single banner Didn't fix the signal

Same duplicate-content problem, nicer look.

Consolidated card (shipped) Works on all 3.5M pages

Fixed SEO + mobile UX at once.

Impact

Indexing +21%

Core signal the duplication was fixed.

Ranking +15%

SERP position recovered.

Checkouts +8%

Clarity beat volume.

Trade-offs

Choice vs. risk 1 real option > 8 fake ones

Consolidation removed the ranking penalty.

Coverage vs. richness Chose universal over flashy

Skipped the teaser table's 1–10% coverage.

Speed vs. alignment Built the case, not just the fix

Slower ship, real stakeholder buy-in.

My role: identified the risk before it shipped, built and presented the SEO + UX case myself, and designed the preferred alternative that stakeholders ultimately approved.

Turning unused sidebar space into a personalized adoption engine

Hotjar showed nearly all dashboard engagement clustered middle-and-left; the right sidebar ran generic news that connected to nothing inside the platform. Replaced it with segment- and behavior-driven product widgets that retire themselves once a user actually adopts what they're showing.

Personalization Behavioral Segmentation Adoption
+10%feature adoption in 2 weeks
Product insight

The sidebar wasn't empty; it was showing content that mattered to no one. The fix wasn't better content, it was matching the right widget to the right user at the right moment.

View how the personalization was built
  1. Diagnose. Hotjar heatmaps and session recordings showed engagement clustered in the middle and left of the dashboard; the right sidebar's generic news feed was effectively unused real estate.
  2. Segment. Mapped each user's existing customer-category tag to the products most relevant to them: funding benchmarks and live deals for VCs, live deals for IBs, profile claimer for founders.
  3. Rotate (v1). Replaced the news feed with 3–4 relevant product widgets per segment (funding benchmarks, newsletters, live deals, Chrome extension, profile claimer), rotating on simple click-based logic to ship and validate the concept fast.
  4. Rotate (v2). Evolved rotation to be driven by the user's recent in-platform searches: someone searching funding-related terms would surface the funding benchmark widget next, instead of rotating blindly.
  5. Deprecate on adoption. Once a user reached 5 sessions using a given product, its widget retired for that user and a new, under-adopted product took its place, so the sidebar kept surfacing whatever the person hadn't discovered yet.

Impact

Feature adoption +10% in 2 weeks

Aggregate lift across widget-surfaced products.

Extension installs +18%

Most-overlooked product, biggest lift.

Newsletter signups +27%

Clearest single action converted best.

Trade-offs

Simplicity vs. depth Shipped clicks before intent

Proved the concept before the complex version.

Exposure vs. pressure Deprecate after 5 sessions

Frees slots for what's still undiscovered.

My role: diagnosed the unused-space problem from Hotjar data, designed the segment-to-product mapping, built both rotation logics, and defined the adoption-based deprecation rule that kept the sidebar constantly working toward under-adopted products.
